# Scarlet Hills

> An original dark fantasy story exploring belief, identity, power, conflict, and the nature of the soul.

## Overview

**Scarlet Hills** is an original dark fantasy narrative project developed through a series of interconnected chapters.

The story follows **Eleanor and Desmond RedWood**, two siblings traveling through a world shaped by supernatural forces, forgotten beliefs, dangerous creatures, and conflicts that extend far beyond their own lives.

What initially appears to be a series of isolated events gradually becomes connected to a much larger conflict involving a powerful religious organization, a false god, traveling hunters, and an international dispute between **Belmonte District** and the **Nation of the Sun**.

The narrative combines:

- Dark Fantasy
- Supernatural Horror
- Mystery
- Action
- Political Conflict
- Worldbuilding
- Character-driven storytelling

---

# The World

The world of **Scarlet Hills** is shaped by the interaction between ordinary human societies and supernatural forces that most people only partially understand.

Different regions have developed their own beliefs, political structures, traditions, and attitudes toward supernatural practices.

As the story progresses, the protagonists become increasingly involved in conflicts that extend beyond individual encounters and eventually threaten entire populations.

---

# Major Conflicts

The narrative is built around several interconnected conflicts.

## The RedWood Siblings

Eleanor and Desmond begin their journey dealing with problems that appear relatively local.

Desmond works as a creature hunter, while Eleanor attempts to understand and control her supernatural abilities.

Their individual experiences gradually expose them to forces much larger than themselves.

---

## The Traveling Hunters

A group of traveling hunters eventually becomes connected through their encounters with the cult and the entity worshipped as a god.

These characters come from different backgrounds and possess different motivations, abilities, and perspectives.

Although they may not initially share the same goals, their encounters with the organization gradually bring them into the same conflict.

The hunters serve as an important counterpoint to the cult:

**They hunt what they do not understand.**

**The cult seeks to control what others believe.**

Their journeys eventually converge around the same threat.

---

# The Cult

A powerful organization operates behind much of the supernatural conflict.

Its members seek to spread the belief in a fabricated god and establish connections between human souls and the entity they worship.

The organization's objectives extend beyond simple religious conversion.

It seeks to manipulate belief itself as a source of supernatural power.

---

# The False God

The entity worshipped by the cult was not originally a god.

It was once human.

Through experimentation, psychological manipulation, torture, and prolonged indoctrination, the individual was gradually convinced that they were divine.

Eventually, the distinction between human identity and divine identity became almost impossible to separate.

The entity's power is connected to the belief of others.

The more people believe in it, the stronger its influence becomes.

This creates a central conflict within the story:

> **Is something truly divine because it possesses power, or because people believe that it is?**

---

# Belmonte District

**Belmonte** is one of the primary regions of the story.

It is a politically and culturally distinct territory that becomes increasingly important as the narrative develops.

The district has its own traditions and religious beliefs, including a strong relationship with the faith surrounding the Sun.

Belmonte is also not particularly welcoming toward foreigners or unfamiliar supernatural practices.

This creates additional tension for characters such as Eleanor and Sophia, whose practices do not necessarily align with the beliefs of the region.

---

# The Nation of the Sun

The **Nation of the Sun** represents another major political power within the setting.

Its interests conflict with those of Belmonte, creating an international dispute that exists independently from the supernatural conflict.

The political relationship between the Nation of the Sun and Belmonte gradually becomes connected to the larger events surrounding the Tower, the cult, and the false god.
